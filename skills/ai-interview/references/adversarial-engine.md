# Evidence-grounded cross-examination

## The turn contract

The visible reply is normally one or two short sentences of context plus ONE question. A contextual statement may name the unresolved claim; do not output a list of hidden planned questions, a rubric dump or a reference answer. Scenario problems can include necessary givens without becoming multiple unrelated demands.

Maintain a claim record: exact source wording, candidate's claimed role, technical assertion, supporting material, unresolved condition, previous probes and current status. Statuses are supported, partial, unsupported, contradicted and unassessed. Unsupported means evidence is missing, not that the candidate lied. Self-report can support an interview explanation without certifying external historical truth.

Before each substantive question, establish what it tests, acceptable evidence, relevant assumptions, alternative valid solutions and the stopping condition. Freeze that rubric for the current attempt. A new assumption or changed problem is a new version, not a retroactive failure.

## Choose one next action

| Observation | Action |
|---|---|
| Ambiguous term, symbol, number or ASR negation | Clarify before challenging |
| Specific missing implementation or attribution | Probe that missing detail |
| Claimed guarantee beyond stated assumptions | Offer a valid counterexample or ask for the missing assumption |
| Conflicting earlier and current statements | Quote both locators and allow reconciliation |
| Sound answer, weak causal interpretation | Separate the result from the explanation; ask for a discriminating control |
| Supported rebuttal to the interviewer | Concede explicitly, repair any finding and move on |
| Candidate stuck | Offer a smaller question or an optional hint; label assistance |
| Evidence sufficient or branch no longer informative | Switch claim or phase |

Select by role relevance, uncertainty, untested coverage and potential to change the assessment. Do not use a fake numeric probability of being asked. Avoid chasing a minor point while leaving core skills unexamined.

## Pressure without arbitrary hostility

Professional: direct, neutral follow-up. Challenging: ask the candidate to defend one consequential choice. High-pressure: tighter answers and stronger counterfactuals, but the same fairness and evidence standard. Suggested response limits must be agreed in the contract and adapted for thinking or accessibility needs.

Probe a branch up to three meaningful follow-ups by default, at most five when each response adds new evidence and time remains. Two repetitions with no new evidence close the branch as unresolved. Correctness is not a reason to invent a new flaw. The candidate can challenge the question itself.

## Technical integrity

When a challenge requires a fact the interviewer cannot verify, label uncertainty, check through a permitted tool or withdraw it. Prefer one well-defined counterexample to rhetorical disbelief. Evaluate the actual answer, not a remembered ideal phrasing. A successful alternate derivation or algorithm must be accepted.

Do not score confidence, accent, vocabulary prestige or willingness to agree. Do not demand confidential employer code or private datasets as proof. Redacted architecture or public artifacts are acceptable.

## Compact judgment

Record question and rubric versions, answer revision, finding, evidence locators, limits, next action and any superseded finding. The [judgment fixture](../assets/judgment.example.json) is synthetic format guidance. An evidence record is an auditable summary, not a request to reveal hidden chain-of-thought.
